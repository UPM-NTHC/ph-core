Connected scenarios

These synthetic datasets show how PH Core representations connect across resources. They are informative examples, not clinical protocols, registry records, PhilHealth claim-submission specifications or additional conformance requirements. Identifiers, contact details and event times are invented for illustration.

The examples are organized by scenario. Juan Dela Cruz (born 1 January 1980) and Miguel Reyes (born 15 March 1981) are different synthetic patients with different example PhilHealth IDs. Repeated clinician names represent illustrative roles, not assertions about actual people.

Primary-care story

Juan's diabetes was reported to have started in 2020 and is documented in the March 2025 follow-up. Dr. Maria Clara Santos works in the PGH outpatient service. The facility is the care provider; the separate illustrative PRC Organization is the credential issuer; the separate illustrative PhilHealth Organization is the insurer/payor in the Claim and Coverage.

  1. Patient, Practitioner, PractitionerRole, PGH and Location identify the participants and setting.
  2. Encounter connects the visit to Juan and PGH. Condition represents the diabetes problem; onset and documentation time are separate.
  3. Fasting-glucose request and HbA1c request identify different requested tests. The completed Task records collection work focused on the fasting-glucose request. The later glucose and HbA1c results link to their respective requests.
  4. Blood pressure and temperature were recorded during the visit.
  5. MedicationRequest renews the antihypertensive medication. MedicationDispense illustrates 30 tablets at one tablet daily, MedicationAdministration records a later taken dose, and MedicationStatement records the report made at this visit of use that began on 10 March. These resource types describe different facts; they do not form mandatory national workflow steps.

The primary-care transaction packages linked records for demonstration; entry order is not an ordered clinical audit log. The Claim example demonstrates generic FHIR reimbursement representation and does not establish PhilHealth benefits or reimbursement amounts.

Historical records and focused examples

Historical observations keep their own event dates. A patient's current follow-up encounter is not reused for an earlier surgery or immunization. The electrolyte panel references sodium and potassium observations in the surgical episode; the glucose test belongs to the later follow-up. The BMI example demonstrates derivation from the linked weight and height.

Bundle-private examples are defined inline in the owning Bundle FSH file and are documented in its resource index. Shared core and focused examples remain separately published and can be reused across scenarios. The two transaction Bundles are intended to include their literal-reference dependencies. Search-response Bundles can reference resources not included in that response, just as a real FHIR search may. They are not transaction requests.

Using the examples

Use the linked generated JSON as the maintained source of each example. Transaction fullUrl values are illustrative correlation URLs, not production endpoints; send a transaction only to a server whose CapabilityStatement supports it. Successful POST responses may assign new logical IDs. Repeating a create transaction can create duplicates, so treat loading/reloading as a server-specific test operation.

See the FHIR REST tutorial for general HTTP exercises. Profile constraints, cardinalities, bindings and designated actor obligations establish conformance; optional content in an example does not introduce a new requirement.

The organization of these examples follows the separation of informative examples and profile guidance seen in the US Core examples and Basic Provenance guidance. PH Core uses its own synthetic scenarios and constraints.
